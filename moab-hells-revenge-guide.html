Hell’s Revenge (Moab) — obstacles & how-tos

Supplement to Moab trails — Hell’s Revenge and Tip-toe through Hell. Text below is synthesized from public tutorials and trail write-ups (see references). It is not a substitute for maps, spotting, your own judgment, or current Sand Flats rules.

Safety & land use: Sand Flats is a designated-route area — stay on marked OHV routes. Obstacles are optional; use bypasses whenever in doubt. Run lockers and low range as appropriate, keep arms and limbs inside the vehicle on off-camber moves, and use a spotter on consequential lines.

Trail overview & access

Hell’s Revenge is a famous slickrock OHV route in the Sand Flats Recreation Area east of Moab, managed under a Grand County / BLM partnership. Guide writers commonly cite about 6.5–8 miles for the loop, roughly 1,200 ft of elevation gain, and about 2–4 hours at a social pace (longer if you queue at obstacles or film).

Fees & booth: Sources agree on a per-vehicle day-use fee at the Sand Flats entrance station (often cited around $5; confirm current pricing at the booth or official pages).

Trailhead / GPS: Multiple guides list coordinates near 38.5756°N, 109.5225°W — entrance just past the pay station on Sand Flats Road.

Difficulty framing: Several authors describe the main loop with bypasses as roughly a “6 / 10” experience, moving toward “8 / 10” if you stack the signature optional obstacles — which matches treating harder spurs like RR4W “6” moves for equipment and spotting (see the trip’s Moab trails card).

Vehicle baseline (typical recommendations): true 4WD, solid ground clearance, 33–35″ tires, and lockers recommended for the full experience; air down for traction and ride quality on Navajo sandstone. Skilled drivers still report doing bypass-heavy laps on more modest setups — at higher risk of body damage or winch work.

Practical tips (from guides & forums)

  • Use bypasses freely. Nearly every hard feature has an easier line; ego is the most expensive mod.
  • Spotter on the spurs. Outside mirrors, a calm second set of eyes, and agreed hand signals beat guessing on V-notches and hot tubs.
  • Watch for cyclists. Hell’s Revenge crosses / nears the Slickrock Bike Trail in places — slow speeds and courteous passing matter.
  • Speed & control: Sand Flats enforces low speeds (commonly cited 5–15 mph depending on sign segment). Momentum is a tool on ledges, but uncontrolled speed on slickrock is how ribs and diffs meet physics.
  • Route marking: Some Moab operators describe following paint dots / cairns on the rock; always cross-check with a current map or GPS track — paint changes over time.
  • When to go: Spring and fall offer cooler temps; mid-summer is brutal on diffs and humans. Major events (for example Easter Jeep Safari) mean crowds and long waits at obstacles.
  • Skills ladder: CS Ginger and others suggest Fins ’n Things first if you want a mellower Sand Flats introduction before committing to Hell’s exposure.
  • Rentals: Some commercial Jeep rentals explicitly forbid Hell’s Revenge and may use telematics — verify your contract before leaving pavement.

Cautionary areas & etiquette (beyond the “big name” obstacles)

  • Entrance fin: Often described as a steep, somewhat narrow one-way climb onto the mesa — not a named “obstacle,” but where anxiety spikes first. Take a breath, pick a straight line, and avoid stopping mid-hill if traction allows.
  • Two-way traffic sections: After the climb, there are wider two-way segments; still watch for oncoming groups and bikes.
  • Emergency / bypass spurs: Some maps show left-side bypasses intended for rescue or easier travel — using them when you are not impeding access is fine, but don’t block them.
  • Mid-trail “cut-across” / shortcut: CS Ginger notes a signed shortcut that skips roughly half the loop — useful if you are behind schedule or weather moves in, but know where you are rejoining the main line.
  • Hell’s Gate spur: The Gate is an offshoot from the main loop — you descend and return; spectators often gather here.
  • Devil’s Hot Tub vs Mickey’s: Multiple writers stress that Devil’s Hot Tub is on private land and is not for public use; the only hot-tub feature generally discussed as a legal optional is Mickey’s Hot Tub (look for its nameplate).
  • Water & slime in tubs: Stagnant water and mud make exits dramatically harder — many roll-overs happen trying to climb out with wet tires on steep walls.
  • Exit fin: CS Ginger warns the exit can still feel “exciting” — do not relax into autopilot just because the famous obstacles are behind you.
  • Tip-Over Challenge in odd conditions: Rocks and Dirt notes that sand on slickrock can make the Tip-Over line unexpectedly slick — if the rock looks dusty, reassess.

Named obstacles & how to work them

All of the features below are optional. Descriptions combine multiple public sources; walk the line on foot first when possible.

Hell’s Gate

What it is: A famous steep V-shaped notch / chute — often described as ~20–45° in places — where wrong tire placement can put you off-balance or backwards. It is widely regarded as one of the most consequential optional moves on Hell’s Revenge.

How people succeed:

  • Spotter mandatory for most private groups — the driver cannot simultaneously watch both front tire placement and rear drop-offs.
  • Tire placement over throttle: Rocks and Dirt emphasizes holes / pockets near the top that can drop a rear tire and pivot the rig into a flop; inch forward only when each wheel has a deliberate target.
  • Straight body, minimal steering correction once committed — large corrections mid-slope load/unload sidewalls unpredictably on slickrock.
  • Lockers & low range so you are not surging to “save” a stall — surges cause rebounds in V-notches.
  • If anything feels wrong: back out early (where safe) or rope off — the audience will not pay your deductible.

The Escalator (“Escalator to Hell”)

What it is: A multi-step slickrock climb in a narrow groove — UTV Guide describes a line that twists and sometimes needs tires pressed to the walls, with moments that can lift a front wheel. It is generally rated a notch below Hell’s Gate in consequences but still serious.

How people succeed:

  • Momentum planning: Know which step needs a gentle bump versus a crawl — “half throttle panic” is how rigs end up at the bottom again.
  • Use sidewalls deliberately only when that is the chosen technique for your vehicle class — understand how UTVs vs solid-axle Jeeps load differently in a V.
  • Bypass on the left per CS Ginger’s mileage note — if you are not looking for tiered groove work, stay on the main loop.

Mickey’s Hot Tub (and hot-tub caution generally)

What it is: A deep sandstone bowl — easy to roll in, often very hard to climb out, especially with water or mud in the bottom. UTV Guide and others note slippery sludge on tires after multiple attempts.

How people succeed (or wisely skip):

  • Do not enter if there is meaningful water unless you have a recovery plan you have actually practiced — winch anchor points, strap direction, and a calm crew.
  • Line on exit matters more than horsepower: Wrong diagonal exits load roll axis and have produced backwards flips in amateur video.
  • Winch / snatch block mindset: CS Ginger notes that wet tubs may require winch or strap assistance even for capable rigs.
  • When in doubt: spectate, photograph, and drive around — Mickey’s will still be there after more seat time elsewhere.

Tip-Over Challenge

What it is: A short, off-camber climb that looks worse than it drives for many built 4x4s — UTV Guide says the name predates modern UTVs and that it is not always as nasty as the label. Still treat it with respect.

How people succeed:

  • Slow, steady throttle — let the tires creep rather than shock-load the sidewalls.
  • Check for sand or debris on the rock face (Rocks and Dirt) before committing; brush off what you can on foot.
  • Bypass to the right in CS Ginger’s ordering — use it if the group is tired or the line looks polished and greasy.

Staircase

What it is: CS Ginger reports the Staircase was closed for many years and reopened around 2022 — a V-entry style problem compared to a “mini Hell’s Gate.” Poor initial line can feel tippy early in the feature.

How people succeed:

  • Walk the V first — decide whether your wheelbase straddles or bridges the low point.
  • Mirror Hell’s Gate discipline: deliberate placement, minimal mid-commit steering, spotter aligned where they can see both front contact and rear departure.

Rubble Trouble (optional spur)

What it is: Listed on some trail databases (for example AllTrails pairing) as an optional harder spur — not always highlighted in every narrative guide. Treat as unknown until you scout it the day-of.

Scenic / cultural stops (not obstacles, still worth noting)

  • Dinosaur tracks — mentioned by CS Ginger near the early part of the loop; stop only where safe and legal, and avoid spinning tires on fragile surfaces.
  • Overlooks — Colorado River, Arches rim, La Sal Mountains views are part of the draw; mind edges and wind when opening doors.

Rough order along the loop (from CS Ginger’s mileage log)

Use this as a mental checklist, not a GPS substitute — distances vary slightly by GPS and spurs attempted.

  1. Entrance climb onto the mesa, then easier two-way slickrock.
  2. ~1 mi: optional bypass on the right skipping some fins / steep descents.
  3. ~2 mi: signed cut-across shortcut opportunity.
  4. ~3 mi: vicinity of the Hell’s Gate turn-off (spur, then return to loop).
  5. ~4.5 mi: Mickey’s Hot Tub area.
  6. ~5 mi: Escalator on the right — main loop stays left to bypass.
  7. ~5.2 mi: Tip-Over Challenge (bypass right) and vicinity of Staircase.
  8. After named challenges: working toward the exit fin — still pay attention.
